Safety — the “S” in RAMS

The other three pillars protect the mission; safety protects people. It asks: can this system harm anyone, and is that risk acceptable?

Definition

Safety is freedom from unacceptable risk, where risk = frequency of a hazardous event × severity of its consequences. Safety engineering identifies hazards, assesses their risk, and reduces it to a tolerable level.

Key ideas

  • Hazard vs. risk — a hazard is a potential source of harm; risk quantifies how likely and how severe.
  • Hazard analysis techniques — PHA, FMECA, FTA (fault trees), HAZOP.
  • SIL — Safety Integrity Levels — SIL 1 to SIL 4, target failure rates for safety functions (IEC 61508 and sector standards).
  • Risk acceptance principles — ALARP, GAME, MEM; safety cases to demonstrate compliance.
  • Safety ≠ reliability — a system can fail often but safely (fail-safe), or rarely but dangerously.

Practical example

A level-crossing barrier that fails closed stops traffic — poor availability, but safe. One that fails open is a hazardous failure. Safety design is about steering failures toward the safe state and making dangerous failure modes extremely rare (SIL targets).
