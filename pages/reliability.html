Reliability — the “R” in RAMS

Reliability answers one question: how long can I trust this system to work without failing? It is the foundation the other RAMS pillars build on.

Definition

Reliability R(t) is the probability that an item performs its required function, under stated conditions, for a stated period of time. For a constant failure rate λ: R(t) = e−λt

Key ideas

  • Failure rate (λ) — how often failures occur, typically in failures per hour.
  • MTBF / MTTF — Mean Time Between Failures (repairable items) and Mean Time To Failure (non-repairable items). For constant λ, MTBF = 1/λ.
  • The bathtub curve — failure rate over a life cycle: infant mortality, useful life, wear-out.
  • Series vs. parallel systems — in series, reliabilities multiply (weakest link); redundancy in parallel raises system reliability.

Practical example

A pump has a failure rate of λ = 2 × 10−5 failures/hour (MTBF = 50 000 h). Over one year of continuous operation (8 760 h): R = e−0.0000175 × 8760 ≈ e−0.175 ≈ 0.84 — an 84 % chance of running the whole year without failure.
